# Shared Reality Gap Taxonomy

## Part 1 — Definition

**Collaboration Intelligence detects and repairs gaps between *perceived shared reality* and *actual shared reality*.**

Temporary teams do not fail because they lack the ability to make decisions or execute tasks. They fail because people believe they share a synchronized mental model of reality when they do not. The engine's purpose is not to manage the team's work, but to reveal the invisible decay of shared reality before execution exposes it too late.

## Part 2 — Gap Classification (Validated Core)

The following gaps represent the fundamental failure modes of temporary collaboration, each corresponding to a specific missing element of reality.

### 1. Ownership Gap
- **Missing Thing:** Decision authority
- **Reality:** Nobody owns the decision.
- **Perception:** Someone else probably owns it.
- **Repair Mode:** `Assignment`
- **Example:** *"Who makes the final call?"*

### 2. Consensus Gap
- **Missing Thing:** Agreement state
- **Reality:** People disagree.
- **Perception:** Everyone is probably okay with this.
- **Repair Mode:** `Alignment`
- **Example:** *"Are we actually agreeing?"*

### 3. Interpretation Gap
- **Missing Thing:** Meaning
- **Reality:** People mean different things using the same words.
- **Perception:** We are discussing the same thing.
- **Repair Mode:** `Definition`
- **Example:** *"What exactly are we building?"*

### 4. Integration Gap
- **Missing Thing:** Interface contract
- **Reality:** Parts expect different interfaces.
- **Perception:** The connection is already clear.
- **Repair Mode:** `Specification`
- **Example:** *"How do these pieces connect?"*

### 5. Execution Gap
- **Missing Thing:** Current state / Momentum
- **Reality:** Progress is blocked and nobody knows what to do next.
- **Perception:** Someone else is probably moving forward or has the answer.
- **Repair Mode:** `Diagnosis`
- **Example:** *"What is actually stopping progress?"*

## Part 3 — Repair Protocol

The universal loop for repairing any shared reality gap:

```
Observe (Capture signals)
   ↓
Reveal Gap (Surface mismatch to the team)
   ↓
Propose Reality Update (Offer the specific Repair Mode)
   ↓
Align (Confirm the update resolves the gap)
   ↓
Commit (Lock the new shared reality)
   ↓
Memory (Store as truth for future context)
```

## Part 4 — Test Criteria

To ensure the engine remains a true Collaboration Intelligence primitive and does not degrade into project management, every future evaluator must answer four strict questions:

1. **What reality is hidden?**
2. **Who believes something different?**
3. **Why would the gap remain invisible?**
4. **What minimum action restores shared reality?**

If a proposed evaluator cannot answer these four questions, it is a management feature—not an awareness primitive—and it does not belong in the engine.

## Part 5 — Composite Gaps (Late Stage Execution)

The final three moments of the hackathon lifecycle do not require entirely new primitives. They are temporal or contextual composites of the core five, passing the Four Questions test by revealing late-stage reality decay.

### 6. Pivot Gap (Interpretation + Execution)
Late in the cycle, the original scope becomes mathematically impossible, but the team remains in silent denial.
1. **What reality is hidden?** The current trajectory will not finish in time.
2. **Who believes something different?** Each individual secretly doubts the timeline, but perceives that the *rest* of the team still expects the full vision.
3. **Why would the gap remain invisible?** Sunk cost fallacy and the social fear of being the one to "give up" or compromise the vision.
4. **What minimum action restores shared reality?** A **Reframe** (Aligning on and committing to a brutally cut-down scope).

### 7. Demo Gap (Ownership + Interpretation)
The code is finished, but the narrative is undefined.
1. **What reality is hidden?** The product has no cohesive story; the "why" is disconnected from the "what".
2. **Who believes something different?** The builders believe the technical achievement speaks for itself; reality demands a narrative.
3. **Why would the gap remain invisible?** The team is hyper-focused on compiling/deployment, treating the presentation as an afterthought rather than a core component.
4. **What minimum action restores shared reality?** **Narrative Alignment** (Assigning a speaker and aligning on the single most important hook).

### 8. Reflection Gap (Memory)
The event ends, and the temporary team disbands.
1. **What reality is hidden?** The implicit trust, working dynamics, and lessons forged during the pressure cooker.
2. **Who believes something different?** The team perceives the source code as the only output, forgetting the human capital generated.
3. **Why would the gap remain invisible?** Post-event exhaustion removes the immediate forcing function for coordination.
4. **What minimum action restores shared reality?** **Memory Commit** (Capturing the shared experience or acknowledging individual strengths before disbanding).
