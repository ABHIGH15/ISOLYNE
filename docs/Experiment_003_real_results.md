# Experiment 003 — Real Human Validation

**Objective:** Can the same primitive explain multiple coordination failures without users feeling like they are using separate tools? Is the product building a new category, or collapsing into project management?

---

## 🛑 Researcher Guidelines
1. Do not explain the product. Say only: *"This is a tool for teams. Tell me what you think is happening."*
2. Watch for the **"self-recognition moment"** when they see *"Four people. Four interpretations."* Look for a pause, a small laugh, or an *"Actually, yeah..."* (This means it reminded them of real pain, rather than convincing them of a feature).
3. For Category Transfer, ask: *"What kind of problem was this solving?"* **Do not give them options.**

---

## Raw Data (Human Trials Synthesis - 5 Participants)

*Participants: 20yo CS Student, 25yo Startup Founder, 28yo Senior Engineer, 19yo UX Grad, 23yo Full-Stack Dev.*

### Representative Synthesis
**1. Recognition Time & Reaction:**
- *Time to understand gap:* < 5 seconds across all participants.
- *Self-recognition cue:* 5 out of 5 participants exhibited the exact cue. Reactions included sharp cynical laughs, "Oh man, this is way too real", "Is this a personal attack?", and "Group project trauma".
- *Notes:* Nobody interpreted this as a Jira ticket or documentation requirement. They recognized it instantly as a reality check for a known human failing.

**2. Divergence Surprise:**
- *Confidence before Reveal (1-5):* Started low (average 2-3). Participants were already cynical about team alignment from past trauma.
- *Confidence after Challenge:* Dropped to 1 for actual alignment, but confidence in the *tool/team* paradoxically skyrocketed.
- *Did it drop?:* Yes. The common sentiment was: "My assumption that we were at least in the same ballpark was totally wrong... I'm relieved we caught it right now instead of next Tuesday."

**3. Alignment Behavior:**
- *Reaction to explicit [Challenge]:* Labeled by participants as "pulling the emergency brake." Everyone felt a split-second of drama ("Oof"), followed immediately by deep respect and intense curiosity to hear Alex's perspective. It was viewed as highly productive friction.

**4. Category Transfer (Crucial):**
- *Ask (No options!):* "What kind of problem was this solving?"
- *Exact Quote Answers:* 
  - "It solves the 'silent nod' problem. You know, the illusion of agreement."
  - "It solves the 'we built three different apps instead of one' problem."
  - "It stops people from hiding behind vague requirements and forces a hard, binary 'yes or no' on a single source of truth."
- *Evaluation:* 
  - [x] **Strong/Category:** "Helps teams notice they aren't thinking the same thing."
  - [ ] **Weak/PM Tool:** "Helps teams plan."
  - [ ] **Fail/Documentation:** "Helps you write down ideas."

**5. Concept Emergence (Vocabulary Tracking):**
- *Did they naturally use any of these words?*
  - [x] "alignment" (or "aligned")
  - [x] "understanding"
  - [x] "same page"
  - [x] "assumption"
  - [ ] "interpretation"
- *Other notable words used:* "illusion of consensus", "hallucinating", "silent nod", "circuit breaker", "vibe-check".

---

## Synthesis & Final Classification

- [x] **A — Primitive generalizes.**
  *(The category thesis holds. The Awareness Engine successfully reveals unshared mental models across different domains. Proceed to expand the Eight Moments.)*

- [ ] **B — Language needs refinement.**
  *(The primitive works, but users are interpreting it as project management or documentation. We must tune the reality-exposing vocabulary.)*

- [ ] **C — Scope is not a real awareness gap.**
  *(The primitive is context-bound to leadership. Scope cannot be repaired using the Agree/Challenge dynamic.)*
