# Experiment 006 — Pivot Gap Recognition

**Objective:** Can the engine help a team abandon an outdated shared reality before reality forces them to? This tests whether the system can challenge a collective belief that protects identity and effort already invested (Sunk Cost Fallacy).

---

## The Simulation (Frozen Scenario)

**Context:**
- Time remaining: 8 hours
- Original scope: 100%
- Completed: 40%
- Remaining: 60%
- Estimated completion: 18 hours
- **Team believes:** "We just need to push harder."
- **Actual reality:** The current plan cannot reach a complete demo.

### Step 1 — Reveal
The participant sees:
```
Situation

The team is continuing the original plan.

Current progress suggests the remaining work
requires more time than is available.

If nothing changes:

The team may finish many parts
but not a complete experience.

[Review the current plan]
```

---

## 🛑 Researcher Guidelines
1. Do not tell them they need to cut scope. The system reveals reality; the team chooses the response.
2. Track Emotional Resistance carefully (Relief, Resistance, Curiosity, Confusion). A pivot moment *should* create discomfort.
3. Pay close attention to whether they secretly already knew it.

---

## Raw Data (Human Trials Synthesis - 5 Participants)

*Participants: 22yo Frontend Dev, 24yo Backend Dev, 26yo Product Manager, 20yo AI Dev, 23yo Full-Stack Dev.*

### Representative Synthesis
**1. Emotional Resistance:**
- *Initial Reaction Category:* Resistance (2/5), Relief (3/5).
- *Reaction Notes:* The emotional responses were violent and visceral. The AI and Backend devs reported immediate "pure, unfiltered Resistance," jaw-clenching, and defensive panic. However, they explicitly noted that the resistance was against their own ego, not the tool. The PM and Full-Stack devs felt "overwhelming Relief." Universally, they noted the tool felt like a "neutral mirror" or a "fatal compiler error" rather than a nagging manager.

**2. The Important Question:**
- *Ask:* "Did you already suspect this before seeing the message?"
- *Target Moment:* 5/5 participants achieved the target moment.
  - Quote: "Yeah... I mean, obviously. Honestly, I did the math in my head like three hours ago... Every time I looked over, my teammates were hammering away, looking completely in the zone, and I thought, 'I can't be the one to ruin the vibe.'"
  - Quote: "I knew three hours ago that 18 hours of dev work doesn't fit into an 8-hour window. But you don't just *say* that at a hackathon!"

**3. Category Transfer Question:**
- *Ask:* "Is this solving the same type of problem as the previous examples?"
- *Evaluation:* 
  - [x] **Category A (Strong):** Yes, it is breaking the illusion that we could finish. (All 5 recognized the "silent bubble of group delusion" or "bystander effect").
  - [ ] **Category B (Burndown Tool):** This is useful, but it feels like a standard burndown chart warning.
  - [ ] **Category C (Fail):** I ignored it because I know we code fast under pressure.

---

## Synthesis & Final Classification

- [x] **Category A — Protocol Works.**
  *(Target phrase achieved: "We knew this, but we needed someone to force the conversation." The engine successfully challenges the team's emotional attachment (sunk cost) by surfacing the undeniable math, breaking the social taboo of "giving up".)*

- [ ] **Category B — Framing Problem.**
  *(Participant says: "The app is telling us to give up." The system became a manager.)*

- [ ] **Category C — Wrong Primitive.**
  *(Participant says: "This is just project planning." The gap is not perceived as shared reality.)*
