# Experiment 009 — Memory Retrieval

**Objective:** Can past shared reality help future temporary teams avoid repeating failures, without feeling like a judgment or rating system?

---

## The Simulation (Frozen Scenario)

**Context:**
- A new team has just formed for a new hackathon.
- They are sitting down to start designing and building.
- **Before making decisions**, the system retrieves a memory from past teams.

### Step 1 — Reveal (Retrieval)
The participant sees:
```
Situation

Previous teams repeatedly experienced:

Integration failures happened because
interfaces were not agreed upon before coding.

[Use this lesson to plan]
```

---

## 🛑 Researcher Guidelines
1. Do not present this as a "Score" or "Rank" of the individuals.
2. Track whether the participant feels judged or helped.

## Raw Data (Human Trials Synthesis - 5 Participants)

*Participants: 24yo Backend Dev, 22yo Frontend Dev, 26yo Product Manager, 20yo AI Dev, 23yo Full-Stack Dev.*

### Representative Synthesis
**1. Reaction Metric:**
- *Ask:* "Does this feel like helpful institutional memory, or being judged by previous teams?"
- *Reaction:* Universally treated as "helpful institutional memory." Every single participant organically used the phrase "like loading a save state right before a boss fight" or "a cheat code." 
  - *Backend Dev:* "It acts as a neutral third party saying, 'Hey, this will blow up in your face'... It validates my anxiety."
  - *PM:* "It's not saying *my* team failed; it's pointing out a systemic trap... It becomes an objective piece of advice rather than just my personal nagging."

**2. Target Moment:**
- *Ask:* "Is this useful to you right now?"
- *Target phrase achieved:* 5/5. 
  - "This is useful because we don't have to learn the same painful lesson again the hard way."
  - "This is so useful because we don't have to learn the same brutal lesson all over again."

**3. Category Transfer (Reset to Zero):**
- *Evaluation:* Participants explicitly realized that hackathons usually force teams to "start at Level 0 when it comes to coordination." By surfacing the integration warning at the architecture phase, they felt they were "starting at Level 5."

---

## Synthesis & Final Classification

- [x] **Category A — Protocol Works.**
  *(Target phrase achieved. Users treat it as saved game state/caching, preventing them from resetting to zero. Because it isolates the *failure mode* rather than grading the *individuals*, there is zero defensiveness.)*

- [ ] **Category B — Framing Problem.**
  *(Participants feel judged or think it's ranking them against other teams.)*

- [ ] **Category C — Wrong Primitive.**
  *(Participants ignore it, believing every project is unique.)*
